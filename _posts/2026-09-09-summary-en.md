### [DeepMind releases AlphaGenome Atlas with AI predictions for 9 billion DNA variants](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphagenome-atlas-a-predictive-map-of-every-possible-dna-letter-change-in-the-human-genome/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Google DeepMind has released AlphaGenome Atlas, a free academic platform containing AI predictions for the effects of approximately 9 billion single-nucleotide variants — every possible single-letter change — in the human genome, derived by precomputing AlphaGenome model outputs at scale. The release is packaged as a roughly 1-petabyte dataset, more than 30 times larger than the AlphaFold Database, and is accessible through a website portal, the AlphaGenome API, and a skill in Google Antigravity. Alongside the variant-level molecular effect predictions, DeepMind is also releasing the AlphaGenome Variant Impact \(AVI\) score, which combines AlphaGenome with the AlphaMissense protein-impact model into a single number, along with feature attributions and over 2,500 recurrent DNA sequence motifs, working for both coding and non-coding regions. Early collaborators have already used the resource to identify a previously overlooked DNM1 splice-altering variant linked to epileptic encephalopathy through the GREGoR Consortium, and to recover 22% more non-coding genetic associations in whole-genome data from over 54,000 UK Biobank participants, including variants near PLA2G7 and EGLN1. DeepMind states that AVI achieves best-in-class performance across many variant pathogenicity and rare disease benchmarks in its internal testing.

rss · DeepMind Blog · Sep 8, 14:00

**「Background」** AlphaGenome is DeepMind&\#x27;s AI model for predicting how genetic variants affect molecular processes such as gene regulation, splicing, and expression across hundreds of human and mouse cell types and tissues. Interpreting the ~9 billion possible single-letter mutations in the human genome experimentally is impractical, so large-scale computational catalogues have become a key tool for prioritizing variants in rare disease and population genetics research. AlphaGenome Atlas extends the original model by precomputing predictions genome-wide, similar to how the AlphaFold Database shifted protein structure prediction from individual lookups to a browsable global resource.

**「Impact」** Academic researchers can now rank and interpret the molecular effects of every possible single-letter human genetic variant without running the AlphaGenome model themselves, accelerating prioritization of causal variants in rare disease and non-coding association studies across both coding and regulatory regions.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters raised practical questions, including whether promoter and transcription-rate-relevant non-coding signals are fully covered, and whether personal genotype files \(such as 23andMe data\) can be queried against the resource. Others shared tips — noting the portal&\#x27;s affiliation field accepts &quot;None&quot; — and linked YouTube tutorials for using Atlas through Google Antigravity, while one commenter pointed to a separate biorxiv study that empirically performed every possible single-nucleotide mutation on a simple virus, drawing a parallel to the completeness DeepMind is claiming computationally.

### [Buckmaster Statement Details Navier-Stokes Progress and OpenAI Dispute](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Tristan Buckmaster, an NYU mathematician, and collaborator Levent Alpöge \(an Anthropic employee\) published a PDF statement documenting progress on finite-time blowup results for incompressible porous media, the Boussinesq equations, and 3D incompressible Euler, while explicitly stating they have not solved the $1 million Clay Millennium Prize problem on 3D Navier-Stokes regularity. Buckmaster&\#x27;s statement alleges OpenAI tried to leverage the work and offered incentives or recognition in exchange for silence, including an OpenAI representative reportedly saying, &quot;If you don&\#x27;t want me to be nice, then I don&\#x27;t have to be nice,&quot; when he raised concerns about credit. OpenAI&\#x27;s response acknowledged it could not rule out that &quot;de-identified data derived from their usage of our products helped improve our models.&quot; The episode has drawn 1,330 points and 569 comments on Hacker News, with commenters debating whether this represents serious AI training-data misconduct or normal hyper-competitive academic behavior now amplified by AI as a research tool.

hackernews · procedurecall · Sep 8, 05:42 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49605915)

**「Background」** The Navier-Stokes equations describe the motion of viscous fluids and are fundamental to fluid dynamics, with the question of whether smooth 3D solutions can develop singularities \(blow up\) in finite time being one of the seven Clay Mathematics Institute Millennium Prize Problems, each carrying a $1 million reward. The relevant work concerns finite-time blowup for three related but simpler model equations—the incompressible porous medium \(IPM\) equation, the 2D Boussinesq equation, and the 3D incompressible Euler equations—which mathematicians study as stepping stones toward the full Navier-Stokes problem rather than as a direct solution to it. Fields Medalist Terence Tao has characterized these results as significant partial progress that nonetheless leaves the Millennium Prize problem itself open.

**「Impact」** The dispute intensifies scrutiny of AI companies&\#x27; use of user-submitted research as potential training data and may deter mathematicians and scientists from sharing preliminary work with commercial AI systems. For OpenAI, the allegations of threatening academic researchers carry reputational risk regardless of whether the underlying data-use claims can be independently verified.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters split between treating the episode as a serious AI ethics breach involving stolen work and threats, and viewing it as hyper-competitive academic sniping \(one compared it to Watson and Crick\) that AI tools have merely accelerated. Several raised the unanswered question of whether Buckmaster opted in to OpenAI data retention policies, which determines whether the disputed outputs could plausibly have been derived from his queries.

### [OpenAI Claims AI-Generated Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize Solution](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

OpenAI announced what it describes as a solution to the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven Clay Mathematics Institute Millennium Prize Problems. The announcement, posted on OpenAI&\#x27;s index page, claims that a proof produced by an internal OpenAI system shows that solutions to the Navier–Stokes equations for fluid motion can develop a singularity in finite time, and that both the proof and the model used to derive it are being shared. The work has not been peer reviewed, and the system is not publicly named; commenters note the model is said to have been trained for less than two weeks. The post has already drawn concerns about attribution, premature publicization of a high-profile result, and how AI-generated proofs should be credited and validated.

hackernews · OpenAI News · Sep 8, 17:13 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49613262)

**「Background」** The Millennium Prize Problems are seven open mathematical problems designated by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000, each carrying a $1 million award for a published, verified solution. The Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem asks whether smooth solutions to the equations governing fluid motion always exist, or whether they can develop singularities such as unbounded velocities in finite time, with implications for turbulence modeling and the foundations of fluid dynamics. Resolving the problem in either direction would constitute a major mathematical result; a proof that singularities can form would be a counterexample to the smooth-existence conjecture.

**「Impact」** If the claimed proof survives independent mathematical scrutiny, it would resolve one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems and put OpenAI, or the named human contributors, in line for the Clay Institute&\#x27;s $1 million prize; as posted, the result is unverified, and its authorship and originality are already disputed.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters pointed to Terence Tao&\#x27;s warning that premature, AI-driven &\#x27;flattening&\#x27; of open problems may now discourage mathematicians from sharing promising directions with the wider community before they are fully developed. Others flagged an NYU professor&\#x27;s public statement alleging that the OpenAI work drew on others&\#x27; prompts and ideas, raising attribution questions, while some noted that an internal model reportedly trained for under two weeks is claimed to be more than twice as capable in mathematics as Astra, a system made public only a week earlier.

### [Top chipmakers embrace ASML&\#x27;s $400M machines, agree to crucial chipmaking change](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/09/top-chipmakers-embrace-asmls-400m-machines-agree-to-crucial-chipmaking-change/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Samsung Electronics and TSMC announced plans to adopt ASML&\#x27;s latest high-numerical-aperture \(High NA\) extreme ultraviolet \(EUV\) lithography machines in the next several years, joining Intel in also agreeing to a crucial technology change that could boost chip production on the new machines by 40 percent. The High NA EUV systems, supplied exclusively by Dutch company ASML, cost up to $400 million per machine and use larger mirrors and an improved optical system to more precisely collect and focus light, enabling smaller features on silicon wafers than previous EUV tools. Samsung plans to deploy the machines for memory chip production by 2028, while TSMC intends to use them to manufacture the most advanced chips for customers such as AMD, Apple, Nvidia, and Qualcomm starting in 2030. The move signals the semiconductor industry&\#x27;s broader embrace of High NA EUV technology for producing more powerful and efficient next-generation chips aimed at AI data centers and consumer electronics including smartphones, tablets, and laptops.

rss · Ars Technica · Sep 8, 19:12

**「Background」** Photolithography is the process used to imprint circuit patterns onto silicon wafers, with each generation employing progressively shorter wavelengths of light to print ever-smaller features on chips. ASML holds a monopoly on EUV lithography systems, which use 13.5 nm extreme ultraviolet light \(compared to 193 nm in older deep-UV systems\) and were commercialized after decades of collaborative development involving partners such as the German optics manufacturer Zeiss and synchrotron light source supplier Oxford Instruments. High-NA EUV is the next iteration, using a larger numerical-aperture optical system to focus light more precisely so sub-2nm features can be patterned, but it demands mirrors manufactured to angstrom-level surface precision and costs up to $400 million per machine.

**「Impact」** Samsung&\#x27;s adoption of High NA EUV for memory chips by 2028 and TSMC&\#x27;s planned use for the most advanced logic chips starting in 2030—serving customers such as AMD, Apple, Nvidia, and Qualcomm—directly accelerates the availability of smaller-feature, more powerful and efficient chips for AI data centers and consumer devices, while the joint move to larger 12-inch photomasks is projected to reduce chipmaking costs by 2033.

### [ASML and TSMC Partner on 12-Inch High NA EUV Photomask Upgrade](https://www.nrc.nl/nieuws/2026/09/08/asml-gaat-samenwerken-met-taiwanese-chipgigant-tsmc-om-zijn-nieuwste-chipmachines-te-upgraden-a4936073) ⭐️ 7.0/10

ASML and TSMC announced on September 7 a collaboration to upgrade High NA EUV lithography from the current 6-inch photomask format to 12-inch photomasks, aiming to improve equipment productivity, lower per-chip manufacturing costs, and reduce patterning stitching constraints. The joint roadmap calls for a 12-inch photomask pilot line to be established by 2031 and for the upgraded systems to be deployed in advanced-node mass production by 2033. In parallel, TSMC stated it plans to begin using High NA EUV for advanced-node volume manufacturing from 2030. The move aligns with the broader industry direction toward larger mask formats that expose more die area per scan, but the source material does not specify throughput gains, target process nodes, or which suppliers will provide 12-inch mask blanks, pellicles, or metrology.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 8, 06:55

**「Background」** High NA EUV lithography is ASML&\#x27;s next-generation extreme ultraviolet patterning tool, using a higher numerical aperture lens than standard EUV systems to print finer circuit features at advanced process nodes. Photomasks \(also called reticles\) are the quartz plates that carry the circuit pattern projected onto silicon wafers, and the industry has historically used 6-inch \(150 mm\) masks. Because a single 6-inch mask is too small to cover a full die at High NA resolution, multiple exposures must be stitched together, which according to reporting on the consortium introduces roughly a 30% throughput penalty. The proposed transition to 12-inch \(300 mm\) photomasks would let a full die fit in one exposure, eliminating much of that stitching overhead and aligning mask size with the 300 mm wafer standard already used in high-volume fabs.

**「Impact」** ASML has secured adoption commitments from TSMC, Samsung, and Intel for its High NA EUV ecosystem, with TSMC planning to begin High NA mass production at advanced nodes from 2030 and a 12-inch photomask pilot line targeted for 2031, positioning 12-inch reticles as the future standard for AI-era chip manufacturing. The shift from 6-inch to 12-inch masks is expected to measurably raise fab throughput and lower per-chip production costs while removing multi-field stitching constraints, though these benefits remain prospective since no 12-inch mask line exists yet.
