### [Spi-Fly algorithm mimics fruit fly olfaction to prevent e-nose forgetting](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/09/just-like-a-fruit-fly-a-new-algorithm-never-forgets-old-scents/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Researchers Kevin Max and Yang Shen at the Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology \(OIST\) have developed Spi-Fly, a neuromorphic algorithm recently published in Neuromorphic Computing and Engineering that enables electronic noses to learn new scents without overwriting previously learned ones. The algorithm is modeled on the olfactory circuit of the fruit fly Drosophila, whose roughly 140,000-neuron brain can identify a wide range of odors in a fraction of a second and retain them long-term. Existing commercial electronic noses—produced by companies such as Alpha MOS, Aryballe, and Odotech for food-quality control, environmental monitoring, and security screening—are typically expensive, limited in the molecular range they detect, and prone to catastrophic forgetting when new odor classes are introduced. By mimicking the fly&\#x27;s combinatorial receptor encoding and downstream neural processing, Spi-Fly offers a brain-inspired, neuromorphic approach aimed at continual learning in chemical sensing.

rss · Ars Technica · Sep 3, 18:22

**「Background」** Unlike vision and hearing, which reduce to a single physical dimension \(wavelength\), olfaction relies on hundreds of receptor proteins that each bind to specific molecular features, producing combinatorial activity patterns the brain must decode—a system whose underlying receptor gene family was only identified in 1991 by Linda Buck and Richard Axel \(Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, 2004\). Commercial electronic noses attempt to replicate this sensing ability digitally but have lagged behind biology in both versatility and memory stability. Fruit flies, despite having brains smaller than a poppy seed, outperform commercial e-noses at sorting and retaining scents, making their olfactory circuit an attractive template for neuromorphic algorithm design.

**「Impact」** For developers of chemical-sensing hardware, Spi-Fly offers a bio-inspired route to continual learning on electronic noses, potentially lowering the retraining overhead and performance degradation that currently constrain deployment in food-quality control, environmental monitoring, and security screening. Its real-world advantage over existing continual-learning techniques remains to be established through broader benchmarking.

### [H Company releases NeoMME multimodal-multilingual encoders](https://huggingface.co/blog/Hcompany/neomme) ⭐️ 7.0/10

H Company has released NeoMME, a family of 260M and 800M parameter multilingual multimodal encoders that process text tokens and raw 32×32 image patches through a single bidirectional Transformer trained from scratch with a masked discrete-diffusion objective, eliminating the need for a separate pretrained vision tower or causal language model. Fine-tuned as NeoMME-Retriever using ColPali-style page-image retrieval, both variants add jointly trained dense \(mean-pooled\) and late-interaction heads, producing both embedding types in one forward pass. On ViDoRe v3 \(nDCG@10\), NeoMME-Retriever-260M reaches 0.523—the highest among models strictly below 800M parameters and within 0.002 of ColQwen2.5 with about 14× fewer parameters—while NeoMME-Retriever-800M reaches 0.556. At a matched 2048×2048 image size on an NVIDIA L40S GPU, the 260M model encodes roughly 51 pages per second, about twice ColModernVBERT&\#x27;s throughput. Hierarchical token pooling and asymmetric quantization reduce late-interaction index storage from about 1.5 MB to 6 kB per page \(a 255× reduction\) while retaining more than 95% of baseline nDCG@10. Both checkpoints are available in Hugging Face Transformers under Apache 2.0.

rss · Hugging Face Blog · Sep 3, 13:13

**「Background」** Most visual document retrievers today are built on top of pretrained generative vision-language models that pair a separately pretrained vision encoder with a causal language model, even for non-generative tasks like retrieval where the decoder overhead is unnecessary. ColPali instead treats document pages as images and produces multi-vector &quot;late-interaction&quot; embeddings inspired by ColBERT, scoring each query token against every document token/patch. NeoMME continues this trajectory by collapsing both modalities into a single from-scratch bidirectional encoder, similar in spirit to ModernBERT&\#x27;s modernization of text encoders.

**「Impact」** For developers building visual document retrieval systems, NeoMME provides an Apache-2.0, single-backbone model that delivers substantially better accuracy-per-parameter on ViDoRe v3 and roughly 255× smaller late-interaction indexes \(6 kB vs. ~1.5 MB per page\), making large-corpus late-interaction search far more practical on existing ANN infrastructure.
