### [Ladybird Releases Its First Installable Alpha](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/23/ladybird/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Ladybird released its first installable alpha browser for macOS and Linux, making its largely independent browser engine available without compiling it from source. It implements core web technologies including HTML parsing, CSS, layout, JavaScript, and WebAssembly, while using external components such as HarfBuzz for text shaping, Skia for 2D graphics, ffmpeg for video, and SQLite; each tab runs in a separate process. Simon Willison reported that most tested sites worked and that WebAssembly-based ffmpeg and Pyodide ran correctly, although Datasette Lite failed because of unsupported service-worker functionality. The project warns that some websites will not work and that the browser may crash. October 2026 development included a new JavaScript JIT, a dedicated thread for style and layout, and an AI-agent-driven gain of 75,650 passing web-platform-tests subtests, bringing Ladybird&\#x27;s reported total to 2,184,722—slightly below Firefox and above Safari—along with a claimed 7x JetStream 3 WebAssembly benchmark improvement.

rss · Simon Willison · Nov 23, 21:46

**「Engine Origins」** Ladybird grew out of the more ambitious SerenityOS project, and many of its components were written for Ladybird itself or brought over from SerenityOS. Unlike earlier source-only builds, the alpha provides macOS and Linux downloads while seeking broad compatibility testing, crash reports, and security scrutiny of its multi-process architecture.

**「Early Testing Opportunity」** macOS and Linux users can now install and test Ladybird without building it, but incomplete site compatibility, an observed service-worker failure, and possible crashes make it an experimental option rather than a dependable daily driver. Its issue tracker and user-controlled crash reports also give developers and security researchers concrete ways to contribute compatibility fixes and hardening.

**「Early Tester Reactions」** Testers were broadly impressed, reporting correct rendering across many ordinary and technically demanding sites, including Proxmox and NoVNC, with only minor complaints such as slightly jerky scrolling; one commenter found NoVNC faster than Firefox. Others raised longer-term concerns about fingerprinting, the need for more granular permissions and user controls, and whether reliance on mature third-party libraries weakens Ladybird&\#x27;s original independent-spirited goals, while its separation from Google&\#x27;s advertising ecosystem was viewed as a positive.
