### [Claude solves 25-year-old NIST elliptic curve seed mystery](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Anthropic&\#x27;s Claude discovered that the seed values for NIST&\#x27;s P-192, P-224, P-256, P-384, and P-521 elliptic curves were generated by the C library&\#x27;s lrand48 random-number function seeded with Unix timestamps rather than by any hash algorithm. The discovery, made in under half an hour, resolves a decades-old cryptographic mystery about how the standardized curve parameters were originally produced, and was corroborated by independent analysis from Filippo Valsorda. The result undermines long-standing speculation that the curves were deliberately weakened with hidden backdoors, though it does not address how candidates were chosen from a stream generated over months of timestamps. The binary curve seed B-163 does not fit the same pattern and remains unexplained, with the simplest hypothesis being a transcription error.

hackernews · ahlCVA · Oct 16, 18:55 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50122800)

**「Background」** The NIST P-192, P-224, P-256, P-384, and P-521 curves are standardized elliptic curves widely used for digital signatures and key exchange, introduced in the late 1990s and published as part of FIPS 186-2 in 2000 with seed values attributed to the NSA \[tool-1-2\]. For roughly 25 years, the origin of those seed constants remained unknown, which fueled speculation that the NSA had deliberately weakened the curves by selecting parameters with hidden structure \(a so-called backdoor\). The C library&\#x27;s lrand48 is a legacy pseudorandom number generator that produces 31-bit outputs from a 48-bit internal state initialized by a seed, and it was commonly seeded in 1990s-era C code from the current Unix timestamp via srand48\(time\(NULL\)\).

**「Impact」** This finding substantially weakens decades of conspiracy theories claiming NIST&\#x27;s primary elliptic curves contain deliberate backdoors, since timestamp-based seed generation is incompatible with a deliberately weakened curve construction.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters reacted with a mix of humor and vindication at the debunking of long-running tinfoil-hat theories, though several noted the analysis only confirms how seeds were generated, not how curves were selected, leaving open the narrow possibility of a search-based attack over timestamps. Others observed that B-163 remains unsolved and may simply reflect a digit typo somewhere between original generation and publication.

The writeup also examines whether finding this kind of flaw inherently requires human reasoning about exploit primitives. **「Background」** OpenBSD is a free, open-source Unix-like operating system long regarded as one of the most security-focused projects, with a culture of continuous manual code review and proactive hardening. A vulnerability in its kernel is especially significant because kernel bugs typically allow local attackers to escalate to the highest privilege level and fully compromise the host. The &quot;37-year-old&quot; framing refers to a logic flaw whose original code predates modern auditing tooling and, according to commenters, was only resolved in NetBSD during a 1999 rewrite of the affected subsystem.

**「Impact」** The research reframes assumptions that long-running code review in heavily-audited projects such as OpenBSD rules out exploitable kernel flaws, and pushes the OS security community toward a more honest accounting of which detection methods — fuzzing, static analysis, or human exploit reasoning — realistically apply to this class of bug.

### [PRIMA retinal implant restores reading vision in advanced macular degeneration](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/16/1145840/prima-implant-restores-sight-macular-degeneration-patients/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The PRIMA retinal implant system, developed by California-based Science Corporation, was approved in Europe in July and is awaiting FDA clearance in the US as the first treatment reported to restore functional vision in patients with advanced age-related macular degeneration \(AMD\). More than 80% of recipients in clinical testing regained the ability to read, do crosswords, and use phones after training sessions. The system pairs a 378-pixel photovoltaic retinal implant with camera-equipped glasses that capture roughly 5-megapixel video at 30 frames per second, convert it into 880-nanometer near-infrared pulses via a handheld processor, and project the signal through the pupil onto the chip, which then stimulates healthy bipolar and ganglion cells to relay information to the brain. Because the implant&\#x27;s photovoltaic cells both drive the stimulation current and harvest light to power the chip, no battery is required, and the design intentionally leverages the eye&\#x27;s surviving circuitry rather than replacing the full visual pathway.

rss · MIT Technology Review · Oct 16, 09:00

**「Background」** Age-related macular degeneration damages the photoreceptors of the central retina and affects more than 5 million people worldwide, producing a central blind spot that no prior therapy has been able to reverse. Because AMD typically spares the inner retinal layers and the optic nerve, the condition is one of the few forms of blindness in which a prosthesis can plausibly interface with the eye&\#x27;s own downstream neurons rather than with the brain. PRIMA is built around that preserved pathway by substituting for only the lost photoreceptors.

**「Impact」** For the more than 5 million people globally living with advanced AMD-related retinal atrophy, PRIMA offers the first regulatory-approved pathway to regain reading-level functional vision, although US patient access still depends on pending FDA clearance.

### [ORL speeds up diffusion language models up to 6.9×, hits 2,788 tok/s on RTX 5090](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1x7qgr0/research_2788_tokenss_on_a_single_rtx_5090_with_a/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers released ORL \(On-Policy Residual Learning\), a post-training method for diffusion language models \(dLLMs\) that they report improves generation speed without sacrificing quality. On a single RTX 5090, the LLaDA-2.1-mini model with ORL and the authors&\#x27; new dlmrt inference engine reaches a claimed peak of 2,788 tokens/s using NVFP4, while an 8× B200 setup is reported at 7,708 tokens/s on DiffusionGemma 26B MoE with NVFP4. ORL combines on-policy residual learning—training on fresh trajectories from the current model rather than stale offline residuals—with an Adjacent Entropy Contraction objective that encourages each denoising step to reduce uncertainty relative to the previous one. On the authors&\#x27; converted QwenDLM-4B model, ORL yields up to 6.2× more tokens per forward pass, approximately 6.9× end-to-end throughput, and HumanEval pass@1 rising from 82.9% to 89.6%, while their single-H100 comparison reports 1,298 tok/s versus 746 tok/s for DFlash and 405 tok/s for EAGLE3 at comparable accuracy. The release is fully open source and includes the paper \(arXiv:2610.20687\), training code, evaluation pipeline, the dlmrt engine \(which captures the entire denoising loop in a CUDA graph and supports NVFP4 on Blackwell GPUs\), and model checkpoints on Hugging Face; all throughput figures are stated as peak single-request \(batch size 1\) results rather than batched averages.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/arty\_photography · Oct 16, 20:03

**「Background」** Diffusion language models differ from autoregressive LLMs by generating and refining many tokens in parallel through iterative denoising steps, offering higher theoretical parallelism but historically requiring more forward passes per output. Speculative decoding methods like DFlash and EAGLE3 instead accelerate autoregressive inference by drafting and verifying multiple candidate tokens. NVFP4 is a 4-bit floating-point format supported on NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs \(including the consumer RTX 5090 and the data-center B200\) that trades some numerical precision for higher throughput and lower memory use.

**「Impact」** For practitioners tracking dLLM inference, ORL plus the open-source dlmrt engine brings single-request diffusion decoding on a consumer Blackwell GPU into the throughput range that speculative-decoding systems previously reported only on data-center hardware; the gains on batched or production workloads remain unverified, since the authors state their numbers are batch-size-1 peak results rather than averages across concurrent requests.

### [Anthropic Engineers Reframe Code Review as Continuous AI Checking](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/16/code-review/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Simon Willison surfaced a passage from an Anthropic engineering blog post by Addy Osmani, Morgan Westlee Lunt, and Qing Wang describing how the Claude Code team now handles code review. The authors argue that &quot;did a human read the code?&quot; is an imprecise proxy for &quot;what could go wrong with this change, and which check—automated or human—is the right one to catch it?&quot; Reframing the question this way led Anthropic to move review from a human approval gate at the end of a pull request to a continuous process that runs alongside writing, more like spellcheck than a checkpoint. In their workflow, most changes have already been reviewed and fixed by Claude at least once before a human reviewer is asked to look, so routine mistakes are caught automatically and human attention is reserved for higher-level questions and scrutiny. The quoted post is hosted on claude.dev and presents the change as a deliberate workflow redesign rather than a swap of one tool for another.

rss · Simon Willison · Oct 16, 22:06

**「Background」** Traditional software code review treats human approval as a required gate at the end of a pull request, using a colleague&\#x27;s sign-off as the main proxy for code quality. AI coding assistants such as Anthropic&\#x27;s Claude can now inspect, critique, and suggest fixes to code as it is being written, which makes it feasible to run review continuously instead of only when a change is submitted. This shifts the role of human reviewers away from catching routine mechanical mistakes and toward questions about architecture, correctness, and design.

**「Impact」** Engineering teams adopting Claude or comparable AI coding assistants can let the assistant review and fix routine issues continuously before any human reviewer is engaged, reserving human reviewer attention for higher-level correctness and design questions. The approach&\#x27;s effectiveness depends on the assistant reliably catching domain-specific mistakes, since humans no longer see those routine checks first.

**「Background」** AI benchmarks are standardized test sets used to measure and compare the capabilities of machine learning models across tasks like reasoning, coding, and question answering. Over the past several years, researchers and practitioners have raised concerns that many widely used benchmarks have become saturated—that is, top models cluster near ceiling scores—while also suffering from data contamination, where test items leak into training data, and from leaderboard gaming, where models are tuned to specific evaluators rather than genuine generalization. These issues have prompted calls, including from the referenced Quanta Magazine subject Moritz Hardt, to reform benchmark methodology with the kind of statistical discipline, contamination controls, and reporting standards expected in other scientific fields.

**「Impact」** If rigorous, science-based approaches to AI benchmarking gain traction, the developers, researchers, and organizations that select and compare models based on standardized evaluations would gain more trustworthy, less gameable measurements of model capability. Progress is still uneven, with current AI evaluations often relying on single-run scores that are statistically unstable and susceptible to contamination or saturation.
