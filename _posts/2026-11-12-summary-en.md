### [DeepMind formally verifies parts of Signal’s cryptography](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/signals-encryption-just-passed-an-ai-powered-mathematical-checkup/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Google DeepMind researchers used its agentic-AI framework AlphaProof Code to formally verify a number of key cryptographic guarantees in the Signal protocol. The four-week proof of concept provides further independent validation of the cryptography protecting communications on platforms such as Signal, Google Messages, and WhatsApp, while demonstrating how AI can reduce the labor involved in formal proof development. The analysis found no new vulnerabilities in the examined portions, and when AlphaProof Code was run on older Signal versions containing known, long-fixed vulnerabilities, it correctly identified that those versions could not be formally verified. The work remains limited in scope: it did not verify the entire Signal mobile app, and researchers say the project is one milestone toward scaling AI-assisted formal verification to broader software.

rss · Ars Technica · Nov 11, 14:08

**「Background」** Formal verification uses mathematical proofs to establish that software or a protocol satisfies precisely defined properties, rather than relying only on testing or manual review. Before agentic AI, specifying those properties and constructing the corresponding proofs was extremely time-consuming, which is why DeepMind’s release of the Signal specification and proofs could support further verification work.

**「Impact」** The project gives researchers reusable artifacts for the verified Signal properties and demonstrates that AlphaProof Code can expose known flaws in older protocol versions. It does not certify the current Signal app or show that AI-assisted formal verification is practical for securing large softwarebases generally.

### [DeepMind launches AlphaGenome 2 for cell-specific variant effects](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphagenome-2-deciphering-how-cells-read-dna-to-better-understand-disease/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

DeepMind announced AlphaGenome 2, a conditional DNA sequence model designed to predict how genetic variants affect gene activity in specific human cell types and states. It processes million-letter DNA sequences one tissue, cell type, or state at a time, supports cross-modality imputation of missing measurements, and predicts gene expression, histone modifications, chromatin accessibility, and DNA methylation. DeepMind says the model was trained on more than 107 million individual cell profiles from human and mouse bulk and single-cell data across 68 tissues, enabling it to identify nearly twice as many expression-changing variants as the previous state-of-the-art model. On the TenK10K dataset covering 28 immune cell types, DeepMind reports 30% causal-variant identification versus 16% and 43% directional gene-effect identification versus 23% for prior approaches; it also computationally reproduced an IBD-associated variant’s selective overactivation of ETS2 in gut macrophages. The model can condition on previously unseen cell states from single-cell RNA-sequencing snapshots without retraining, including a COVID-19 vaccine study where a severe-COVID-19-linked variant had a stronger effect in activated T cells; it is available through the AlphaGenome API for non-commercial research and in private preview for commercial research, but the announcement provides no independent validation.

rss · DeepMind Blog · Nov 11, 15:00

**「From broad tissues to cell states」** DeepMind’s original AlphaGenome modeled how DNA sequences and variants affect regulatory activity across broad tissues, and AlphaGenome Atlas extended this work with predictions for all 9 billion possible single-letter changes in the human genome. AlphaGenome 2 replaces parallel tissue-wide modeling with a conditional architecture that treats a tissue, cell type, or cellular state as an input, allowing the same DNA region to be interpreted differently across biological contexts. This progression aims to connect regulatory variant effects with the specific human cell types in which disease-related activity occurs.

**「Research impact」** Researchers can use AlphaGenome 2 to prioritize cell-type-specific regulatory variants and connect enhancer variants to target genes across hundreds of human cell and tissue conditions, potentially reducing the number of disease-relevant variants missed by broad-tissue models. It is available for non-commercial research through the AlphaGenome API and in private preview for commercial research, although its reported gains—including identifying 30% versus 16% of causal variants on TenK10K—have not yet received independent validation.
